Thesis Writeup Model

Executive Summary

SHORT SPOT with a $255.65 PT and 45.9% downside. Spotify is priced at 20.5x 2027E EV/EBITDA because the Street expects a long runway of double-digit growth and margin expansion. Our research argues the earnings algorithm is already getting harder: Premium growth fell to 9% and annual net adds fell 25%; the 2023–25 margin inflection came from a finite restructuring cycle; audiobook bundling is essentially engineered around U.S. royalty rules now under MLC discovery; recent EPS comparisons are polluted by non-operating note marks and tax effects; and approximately €1bn of buybacks did not really reduce the share count as marketed.

We model 10–11% revenue growth across Q4-26E to Q2-27E and value SPOT at 12.0x EV/EBITDA, still a 23.6% premium to the ex-SPOT comp median, implying $255.65 per share.

Business Overview

Spotify is the world’s largest audio-streaming platform, monetizing users through Premium subscriptions and advertising on its free tier. Its premium multiple has recently been justified by rapid income expansion: operating income improved from -€446m in 2023 to €1.37bn in 2024 and €2.20bn in 2025, driven by both revenue growth and cost savings.

Thesis I. Spotify Is No Longer a Growth-Stage Business, but Is Still Valued Like One

Spotify trades at roughly 20x 2027E EV/EBITDA versus 14.5x for Netflix, 9.4x for Warner Music and 4.1x for Tencent Music. But Premium growth fell from 17% in Q2 2023 to 9%, while annual net subscriber additions fell 25% from 32m to 24m. Advertising offers little relief: ad-supported users grew 14% while advertising revenue grew just 1%.

With 777m MAUs and 300m Premium subscribers, Spotify increasingly needs pricing rather than new users to carry earnings growth. That is difficult because Spotify does not own exclusive core content. Our scrape found 57 cancellations following repeated price increases, while transfer tools make switching cheap.

Thesis II. Spotify’s Margin Inflection Was a One-Time Cost Reset, Simply Not Repeatable

The 2023–25 profit inflection was driven by a finite restructuring cycle. Average headcount fell from 9,123 to 7,287, podcast spending was rationalized, real-estate and contractor costs were cut, and 2024 lapped €212m of restructuring charges. The contribution of absolute Opex reduction to incremental operating profit collapsed from 27% in 2024 to 7% in 2025 and 2% in H1 2026.

Reported expenses now mask the reversal. Spotify reported 2025 Opex down €61m, but €165m of lower share-price-linked payroll taxes and a €12m equity-grant timing benefit imply underlying Opex actually increased by roughly €116m. In H1 2026, favorable share-linked social-cost movements masked higher cloud/AI spend, marketing, free trials and legal costs.

Thesis III. Spotify’s Audiobook Bundle Is Essentially for Royalty Engineering, to Be Revealed by the MLC Case

Spotify added 15 monthly audiobook hours to Premium in November 2023, but waited five months—until it created the $9.99 U.S.-only Audiobooks Access tier—to begin treating Premium as a bundle. Basic Individual cost $10.99 and Premium Individual $11.99, implying customers paid only about $1 incrementally for the same audiobook hours assigned a $9.99 reference price for royalty purposes. MLC alleges this reduced the share of Premium revenue attributed to music from 100% to as little as 37%.

Audiobooks-in-Premium expanded to 22 markets, yet Audiobooks Access remained U.S.-only, where the statutory bundling formula creates the royalty benefit. A meaningful portion of recent music-margin improvement may therefore reflect royalty engineering rather than repeatable operating leverage. The surviving MLC case puts Spotify’s internal valuation work, projections and licensing-finance communications into discovery.

Thesis IV. Headline EPS Growth Is Inflated by One-Off Accounting Comparisons

Q2 2026 diluted EPS was €2.61 versus a €0.42 loss in Q2 2025. But Q2 2025 included a €421m non-cash fair-value loss on Spotify’s Exchangeable Notes. Excluding only that item lifts Q2 2025 net income from -€86m to roughly €335m, or approximately €1.6 per share. On that basis, Q2 2026 EPS growth was closer to 60–65%, broadly consistent with the 61% growth in operating income rather than the apparent loss-to-profit explosion.

The distortion flips against Spotify in Q3: Q3 2025 benefited from a €197m fair-value gain on the notes and a €202m tax benefit. As these one-offs roll off, reported EPS growth can decelerate sharply even while revenue and operating profit continue growing.

Thesis V. Spotify Spent ~€1B on Buybacks Without Reducing the Share Count

Spotify spent approximately €439m on open-market repurchases in 2025 and another €547m in H1 2026, or roughly €986m total. Yet ordinary shares economically outstanding increased from 203.84m at YE2024 to approximately 205.58m by June 2026 as option exercises and RSU releases replenished treasury shares.

Recent history suggests a material portion of buybacks functions as anti-dilution rather than true share-count reduction. Future EPS growth must therefore come overwhelmingly from operating-income growth precisely as Spotify’s cost reductions are exhausting and its underlying expense base begins to reaccelerate.

Risks & Mitigants

Risks include stronger subscriber growth and pricing, continued margin expansion, a favorable MLC resolution and future buybacks finally reducing the share count. The valuation does not require an adverse MLC ruling because the growth, cost and accounting theses stand independently.

Catalysts

Q3 2026 creates a difficult EPS comparison against Q3 2025’s €197m note gain and €202m tax benefit. Subsequent quarters should show whether revenue growth normalizes toward 10–11% while reinvestment keeps Opex elevated. MLC discovery can surface internal evidence around audiobook valuation and licensing economics. Continued repurchases without a falling share count would further expose buybacks as anti-dilution rather than EPS-accretive shrinkage.
